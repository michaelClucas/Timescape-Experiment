### What I did

Read the inputs before computing anything (`wp4_inspect_inputs.py`), because
the survey mask is an undocumented pickle and a volume fraction computed
against a misread mask is wrong in a way that looks right. It is a 3-tuple:
a `(360, 180)` boolean sky grid, a resolution integer, and radial limits
`[0, 332.386]` h^-1 Mpc. The indexing convention `[ra*res, (dec+90)*res]` was
then validated by checking that all 1163 VoidFinder void centres fall inside
the mask; the transposed convention puts only 17% inside.

Then computed the fraction under all three finders, both cosmologies, and
both edge treatments, per cuts 2, 3 and 4.

### What came out

**VoidFinder reproduces, and is the only one that does.** Rasterising the
union of all ~40,000 hole spheres onto a 1 h^-1 Mpc grid, which is the
catalogue paper's own method:

| | Planck2018 | WMAP5 |
|---|---|---|
| edge included | **62.28%** | **62.63%** |
| edge flag 0 only | 44.82% | 44.86% |
| edge flag != 1 | 45.86% | 45.72% |
| published (arXiv:2202.01226 Table 6) | 60.8% | 61.1% |

Agreement to about 1.5 percentage points, consistently high in both
cosmologies, which is attributable to the 1-degree mask resolution giving a
slightly different survey volume from theirs.

**The edge treatment moves the answer by 17.4 percentage points**, from 62.3%
to 44.8%. That is far larger than the difference between the two cosmologies,
which is 0.35 points. Cut 4 anticipated this systematic and it turns out to
dominate everything else about the measurement. The edge-excluded number is
not published anywhere I found; it is this cycle's new output.

**A note on the paper.** Table 6's note says "Edge voids are excluded from all
properties except Nvoid", but the published 60.8% matches my edge-INCLUDED
62.3% and not my edge-excluded 44.8%. Section 4.2 describes the method as a
grid-particle count over the void spheres with no edge filter, which is
consistent. So the volume column does not in fact exclude edge voids, despite
the note. Recorded because anyone reading Table 6 at face value would take the
published fraction to be edge-excluded and it is not.

### The negative, and it is the important part of this cycle

**The V2/VIDE and V2/REVOLVER volume fractions are not reproducible from the
published catalogue files.**

| | computed | published | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| Planck2018 V2/VIDE | 73.69% | 68.1% | 1.082 |
| Planck2018 V2/REVOLVER | 80.69% | 90.8% | 0.889 |
| WMAP5 V2/VIDE | 74.33% | 63.0% | 1.180 |
| WMAP5 V2/REVOLVER | 80.66% | 86.5% | 0.932 |

`radius` is confirmed to be the effective radius from the void volume: the
tabulated triaxial semi-axes satisfy `a1 a2 a3 = radius^3` to machine
precision in all 1049 V2 voids across both finders, so the axes carry shape
only and `sum (4/3) pi r_eff^3` is the catalogue's own total void volume.

The partial explanation: arXiv:2202.01226 Sec. 3.2 says V2 "assigns a volume
of 0 to any cell that extends outside the edge of a survey", so `r_eff` is
built from a tessellation volume with boundary cells zeroed, not from the
spatial extent of the void region. Recomputing the spatial extent needs the
Voronoi tessellation, which needs the galaxy positions; the `galzones` files
carry only `NSAID` and zone membership, and the NSA galaxy catalogue is not
part of this dataset. **So the tessellation cannot be rebuilt here.**

But that explanation predicts a one-directional shortfall, and the measured
discrepancy is not one-directional: VIDE computes high, REVOLVER low. So it is
**not fully explained and is recorded as unexplained** rather than reconciled.
The script pins this as a check that asserts the disagreement, so a later
change that accidentally made it go away would fail.

**Consequence for WP-4, and it is a real narrowing.** The plan's "counts
against" clause for WP-4 reads "the measured fraction, with algorithm
spread, excludes the fitted values". The algorithm spread is precisely what
cannot be independently established from this dataset: two of the three
finders' fractions are not reproducible here. What is independently verified
is one finder's number, 62.3% edge-included and 44.8% edge-excluded.

## Cycle 3 - WP-4b, the comparison and K5

### What I concluded

**K5 does not fire.** The measured interval across every verified variant is
0.448 to 0.626, and two fitted `f_v0` values lie inside it: 0.550 and 0.557,
the two CMB matching prescriptions of arXiv:1410.3470 Table 2. The criterion
requires the measurement to exclude *every* fitted value. It does not.

It also could not have fired, for three independent reasons, and this is the
more useful statement:

1. The measured quantity is a **lower bound** on `f_v0`, not an estimate.
   Timescape's phase boundary is a critical-density criterion - arXiv:1306.3208
   p. 2, bound systems "necessarily form in regions where the density is
   greater than critical"; arXiv:1009.5855 Sec. 2, "regions of greater than
   critical density, which are contained within walls". VoidFinder's criterion
   is the absence of wall galaxies, with no density threshold anywhere in it
   (arXiv:2202.01226 Sec. 3.1). Galaxy absence implies sub-critical density on
   any reasonable bias model; the converse fails. So the VoidFinder region set
   is contained in the below-critical set and 62.3% is a floor. A floor of
   0.626 does not exclude a fitted 0.791.
2. The edge systematic, 17.5 points, is comparable to the entire fitted spread
   of 24 points. A measurement whose own dominant systematic is most of the
   range in dispute cannot exclude anything inside it.
3. K5's wording leans on "algorithm spread", which cycle 2 established cannot
   be independently obtained from this dataset.

**But WP-4's "counts against" clause has a second limb and it holds
decisively.** "...or the fitted spread 0.550-0.791 is irreducible."

The sharpest evidence is internal to one paper. arXiv:1410.3470 Table 2 fits
the same Planck data three times, differing only in how the timescape geometry
is matched to the CMB, and gets `f_v0` = 0.627, 0.550, 0.557 with errors of
about ±0.016. Weighted about their mean, chi2/dof = 9.14 on 2 dof; the widest
pair is 3.74 sigma apart. Across the whole literature the 0.550-0.791 spread is
15 times the tightest quoted error, and the full range reaches 0.95.

So **objection O4 hardens, but through the second limb, not the first.** The
distinction matters and I have kept it explicit: K5 firing would have meant
"observation contradicts the model". What is established is that observation
does not currently constrain this parameter at all, and the model's own
determinations of it are mutually inconsistent.

### SOURCE.md field 7's worry, quantified and corrected

Field 7 treats the LCDM conditioning as the headline problem. It is real and
it is about eight times smaller than the edge systematic.

`f_v` is a ratio of volumes, so a uniform rescaling of all distances cancels
exactly. What survives is the change in the *shape* of `D_C(z)` over
`0 < z < 0.114`, set to leading order by `q_0`. The dataset supplies a measured
sensitivity: Planck2018 (`q_0 = -0.528`) against WMAP5 (`q_0 = -0.613`) is
`dq_0 = 0.086` and moves the answer 0.35 points. Timescape's dressed `q_0` at
`f_v0 = 0.695` is `-0.036`, so `dq_0 = 0.492`, a factor 5.7 larger. Scaling
gives **~2.0 points**.

`[INFERENCE]`, premises stated in the script: linearity in `dq_0` over this
range, and `q_0` capturing the leading shape difference at `z < 0.114`. It is
an extrapolation by a factor of 5.7 and is quoted as an estimate. Field 7 was
right in principle; the effect is small *for this dataset* because the survey
is shallow and `f_v` is a ratio. On a deep survey it would not be.

### cuts.md's open question, decided

`cuts.md` deliberately left open whether to recompute under the timescape
distance-redshift relation. **Decided: not done.** The reason is not budget.
The void finders identify voids *using* the comoving galaxy positions, so
changing the distance relation requires re-running the finders. Converting the
published void radii to a timescape scale would move the voids without moving
the galaxies - a different calculation producing a number that looks like the
answer and is not one. Re-running needs the NSA galaxy catalogue, which is not
in this dataset, and is a work package rather than a cycle.

No change was made to `cuts.md`; nothing it decided in advance was relaxed.
Filed as **Q-2** with the reversible default and the recommendation.
