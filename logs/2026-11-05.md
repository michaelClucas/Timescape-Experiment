### What I concluded

**The joint fit is like-for-like and it confirms.** DES fits timescape with one
free cosmological parameter (`f_v0`, `H_0` as nuisance) against flat LCDM with
one (`Omega_m`, same nuisance treatment). Confirmed arithmetically, not from
the prose: using DES's own `AIC = 2k - 2 ln L` and `chi2 = -2 ln L`, the
published `chi2` values reproduce the published `half-Delta-AIC` in both rows
at `Delta_k = 0`, and a one-parameter mismatch would miss by twice the
rounding. `Delta_AIC = 12.6` against timescape on supernovae + BAO. The
`half-dAIC` sign convention Run 01 flagged as unconfirmed is confirmed
verbatim from the Table 7 caption.

**The `H_0` comparison is not like-for-like, on timescape's own criterion.**
Smale & Wiltshire Sec. 2 require `H_0` to be determined above `z = 0.033` by
methods not calibrated below it; SH0ES is Cepheid-calibrated at `z < 0.01`.

**And it is not a rescue either.** The same section states an admissible band
`57 < H_0 < 68` and says a SH0ES-level value "would represent a severe
challenge to the model" - the authors' own words, not an outside objection.
Current SH0ES `73.04 +/- 1.04` is 4.85 sigma above their ceiling. But the same
section predicts up to 17% local enhancement at `z ~ 0.01`, and the excess
needing explanation is 18.6%. Applying the full 17% leaves a residual gap of
0.93 sigma. The discrepancy and the model's own predicted variance are
degenerate at current precision, so **the `H_0` row cannot decide anything by
itself; it collapses into WP-8**, which has never been done.

**Three Hubble constants, not one.** bare `50.1 +/- 1.7`, dressed
`61.7 +/- 3.0`, and `H_bar_tau_0 = gamma_bar_0 H_bar_0 = 67.5 (+3.4/-3.5)`,
spanning 35%. Wiltshire's Comment states it is `67.5`, not `61.7`, that matches
the FLRW `H_0` from the same CMB data. The discrimination table compares the
dressed value to the distance ladder without establishing that the dressed
value is what a ladder determines. That is the whole comparability question and
the answer is that it is not established.
