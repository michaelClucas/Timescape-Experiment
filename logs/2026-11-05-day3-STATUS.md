   writing, before running anything, and was wrong by a factor of six.** The
   reasoning: `f_v` is a ratio, so a mask change moves numerator and denominator

   magnitude, and it names the term I failed to evaluate — what survives

   Per rule 6, four checks encoding the prediction were **inverted to assert

   mask, since it touches only the denominator and has no cancellation to rely

## 8. What I would have done differently with one question answered

**The question: has anyone measured a void volume fraction against a survey
mask whose footprint convention is stated?**

Everything in §11 reduces to one shape. The measured fraction is a floor, the
floor carries a ±1.5-point uncertainty that comes entirely from not knowing
where a survey's edge is drawn to within one degree of sky, and whether one of
the three CMB fits of arXiv:1410.3470 survives turns on exactly that. The
uncertainty is not statistical and not physical — it is a convention nobody
wrote down. One catalogue paper stating its mask convention, or one reading of
VAST's mask code, converts a 3-point bracket into a number and makes that one
exclusion decidable.

I would have spent today's single cycle reading that code rather than
bracketing it, and the day would have ended with three of three prescriptions
decided rather than two.
