## 2. Kill criteria tested, and the evidence

### K1 - tested, does not fire

K1 needs **both** published comparisons confirmed like-for-like and
significant, **and** WP-2 finding the supernova fit not circular.

**The joint fit is like-for-like and it confirms.** DES fits timescape with one
free cosmological parameter (`f_v0`, `H_0` nuisance) against flat LCDM with one
(`Omega_m`, same nuisance). Established arithmetically rather than from the
prose: using DES's own `AIC = 2k - 2 ln L` and `chi2 = -2 ln L`, the published
`chi2` reproduces the published `half-Delta-AIC` in both rows at `Delta_k = 0`,
and a one-parameter mismatch would miss by twice the table's rounding.
`Delta_AIC = 12.6` against timescape on supernovae + BAO. The sign convention
Run 01 flagged as unconfirmed is confirmed verbatim from the Table 7 caption.

**The `H_0` comparison is not like-for-like, and has now failed to decide by
three independent routes.**

1. Timescape's own criterion excludes it. Smale & Wiltshire Sec. 2 require
   `H_0` determined above `z = 0.033` by methods not calibrated below it;
   SH0ES is Cepheid-calibrated at `z < 0.01`.
2. The escape is the same size as the discrepancy. The same section calls a
   SH0ES-level value "a severe challenge" and gives an admissible band
   `57 < H_0 < 68`, which current SH0ES exceeds by 4.85 sigma. But it also
   predicts up to 17% local enhancement at `z ~ 0.01`, against an 18.6% excess
   to explain. Applying the full 17% leaves 0.93 sigma.
3. The method timescape itself endorsed does not settle it either. Strong-lens
   time delays give 73.3 (+1.7/-1.8) with a parametric lens mass profile and
   67.4 (+4.1/-3.2) with it relaxed hierarchically - a 7.1 km/s/Mpc swing,
   larger than the 5.3 gap from timescape's ceiling. And all time-delay values
   assume flat LCDM in the inference, so none is model-independent.

**Second limb.** WP-2 is out of scope and unstarted, and the available evidence
runs against it: DES names its flat-LCDM bias-correction step as the biggest
risk in its own pipeline. So K1 could not have fired today whatever WP-1 found.

### K5 - tested, does not fire, and cannot on this dataset

Measured interval across every verified variant: 0.448 to 0.626. Two fitted
`f_v0` values sit inside it (0.550 and 0.557). K5 needs the measurement to
exclude every fitted value.

It also could not have fired:

1. **The measured quantity is a lower bound, not an estimate.** Timescape's
   phase boundary is a critical-density criterion; VoidFinder's criterion is
   the absence of wall galaxies, with no density threshold in it at all. Galaxy
   absence implies sub-critical density; the converse fails. So VoidFinder
   voids are a subset of the below-critical volume and 62.3% is a floor. A
   floor of 0.626 does not exclude a fitted 0.791.
2. The edge systematic, 17.5 points, is comparable to the whole fitted spread
   of 24 points.
3. K5's wording leans on an algorithm spread this dataset cannot
   independently supply (see §4).

**What did hold is the second limb of WP-4's "counts against" clause.**
`f_v0` is not a measurement. arXiv:1410.3470 Table 2 fits one Planck dataset
three ways, differing only in matching prescription, and gets 0.627, 0.550,
0.557 with errors of ±0.016: `chi2/dof = 9.14` on 2 dof. Across the literature
the 0.550-0.791 spread is 15 times the tightest quoted error and the range
reaches 0.95. Objection O4 hardens through this limb, not through K5.

## 7. What I would have done differently with one question answered

**The question: is `f_v0` defined as the volume fraction below the critical
density, or below some other threshold, and is there a published statement of
the threshold?**

This is the hinge of WP-4 and I resolved it by inference from two prose
sentences in two papers - arXiv:1306.3208 p. 2 and arXiv:1009.5855 Sec. 2 -
neither of which states a threshold as a number or an equation. Both say bound
systems form in regions "of greater than critical density" contained within
walls. That is enough to establish the *direction* of the mismatch, which is
what makes the measured fraction usable as a one-sided bound, and it is not
enough to establish the *size* of the gap.

With a numerical threshold I could have computed the fraction of volume below
it directly, from the stacked density profiles plus the survey's galaxy
density field, and compared like with like. WP-4 would then have produced an
actual test of D4 rather than a bound plus a demonstration that the fitted
values disagree with each other. K5 would have become decidable instead of
undecidable, in either direction.

As it stands WP-4's strongest output is not about the measurement at all: it
is that the model's own fits for `f_v0` are mutually inconsistent at nine
times the scatter their error bars permit. That is a real result and it is
the second limb of the plan's own criterion. But it is a result about the
literature rather than about the universe, and one answered question would
have bought the other kind.
