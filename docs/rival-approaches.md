# Rival approaches (seed, v0)

Timescape is one response among several. An audit that establishes
timescape is self-consistent and fits the data has not established much
unless it also says what else fits, and what would distinguish them.

For each rival the audit records: the mechanism, what it predicts that
timescape does not, and the current constraint status.

---

## Within inhomogeneous cosmology

**LTB / large local void models.** A spherically symmetric
underdensity with the observer near the centre reproduces the
distance-redshift relation without dark energy. Strongly constrained,
notably by kinetic Sunyaev-Zel'dovich measurements and by the Copernican
awkwardness of requiring a near-central position.
Audit: do those constraints transfer to timescape? Make the argument
explicitly either way.

**Swiss-cheese and perturbative backreaction models.** Exact
inhomogeneous solutions glued into an FLRW background. Generally find
small effects.
Audit: whether the smallness is a result or a consequence of the
construction.

**Buchert averaging without the clock effect.** Scalar averaging with
backreaction but no quasilocal-energy clock-rate postulate. This is the
closest rival and the most informative comparison, because it isolates
D6: it shares D1-D5 with timescape and drops the distinctive step.
Audit: what does backreaction alone achieve without D6, quantitatively?
This single comparison is worth more than any other in this document.

**Rasanen's separate-universe / peak-structure averaging.** A different
route to the same question.
Audit: whether it agrees with timescape on magnitude.

## Outside inhomogeneous cosmology

**LCDM.** The incumbent. Six parameters, fits nearly everything, has no
account of what the cosmological constant is and faces the H0 and S8
tensions.
Audit: state the current status of those tensions honestly, in both
directions. They are sometimes overstated by alternative-model advocates
and sometimes dismissed too quickly by defenders.

**Dynamical dark energy.** Evolving equation of state. Recent BAO results
have been read as favouring evolution over a constant.
Audit: establish what the current data actually support, with the
caveats the analysis teams themselves state. If dark energy appears to
evolve, that bears on timescape in both directions and the audit should
say how.

**Modified gravity.** f(R) and others. Produces accelerated expansion by
changing the gravitational action rather than the matter content.
Audit: brief. Note what distinguishes it observationally from timescape.

## The discrimination question

The output the audit should drive toward: a short list of measurements
where timescape and each rival predict *different* values, with the
current precision and the precision needed.

This is more valuable than any amount of fit comparison. A model that
fits as well as LCDM with similar parameter counts is not thereby
preferred; a model that predicts something LCDM does not, and is right,
is a different matter.

Known candidates to assess, all `[LEAD]`:
- Variance and anisotropy in the locally inferred expansion rate.
- The redshift at which apparent acceleration begins.
- Deviations in the distance-redshift relation at intermediate z.
- Any difference in the CMB or BAO calibration that current or near-term
  surveys could resolve.

For each, state whether existing data already discriminates, and if not,
what would.
