## Prompt

Audit the timescape cosmology: establish what it claims, what kind of
claim each piece is, where the weight of the argument sits, and how well
supported each piece is.

`[SEED-ONLY]` `docs/derivation-chain.md` and `docs/objections.md` are
hand-written starting guesses. They were written without retrieval, the
step decomposition may be wrong, and the `kind` tags are likely wrong in
places. Correcting them is a valuable output. Do not treat them as ground
truth.

### What to produce

**Part 1 - The chain.** The actual derivation from "the universe is
inhomogeneous" to "the apparent acceleration is an artifact", as the
primary sources present it. Add, remove, split, or reorder steps where the
seed has it wrong. For each step:

- The statement, precisely.
- Its kind: theorem, scheme, postulate, fit, or interpretation, with a
  confidence tag and the source that settles it. Flag explicitly where you
  disagree with the seed's tag.
- What supports it.
- The strongest published objection.
- **How much of the conclusion collapses if this step fails.** Score 0-3.

The distribution of that last score across the chain is the main result of
this run. If the weight concentrates on one postulate, say so plainly. If
it is spread across well-established steps, say that instead.

**Part 2 - The ledger.** Fill `docs/claim-ledger.md` entries for every
empirical claim you can source, covering at minimum: Type Ia supernovae,
CMB acoustic peaks, BAO, local Hubble flow variance, the Hubble tension,
and structure growth / BBN consistency. Fields C1-C10 as defined there.

C4 and C5 are the ones summaries omit and the ones that decide how much
the headline supernova evidence is worth. For each supernova analysis,
establish from the method section how light-curve standardisation
parameters were treated, and whether a calibration derived under a
standard-model assumption was inherited. Quote the relevant text. If you
cannot establish it, say so explicitly rather than leaving it out.

Never report a comparison statistic without the dataset, the cuts, the
priors, and the free-parameter counts on both sides. Where the counts are
unavailable, record the statistic as uninterpretable.

**Part 3 - The live objections.** For each objection in
`docs/objections.md`, plus any you find: both positions from primary
sources in their own terms, the precise assumption each side says is doing
the work, what the disagreement turns on, and whether anyone has conceded
anything in later work.

Give no verdict on O1 (backreaction magnitude) or O2 (the gauge status of
the clock effect) unless you can show the field resolved them, with a
locator. "Unresolved" is the expected and correct answer. O1 and O2 are
independent: granting one in timescape's favour does not help with the
other, and the output should make that explicit.

**Part 4 - Rivals and discrimination.** Per `docs/rival-approaches.md`.
Prioritise one comparison above the others: **Buchert averaging with
backreaction but without the clock-effect postulate.** It shares the first
several steps with timescape and drops the distinctive one, so what it
achieves quantitatively on its own isolates exactly what the clock effect
is buying. Establish that number if it exists, and say so if it does not.

Then the discrimination table: observables where timescape and each rival
predict different values, with current precision and precision needed, and
whether existing data already decides it.

**Part 5 - Seed corrections.** Everything in the seed docs you found to be
wrong, with sources. A long list here is a good outcome.

**Part 6 - Open questions and unverified leads.** Ranked, each with why it
matters and what would settle it. Include anything you believe but could
not source, with the retrieval that would confirm it.

### Deliverables

`OUTPUT.md` with the six parts in order, and `OUTPUT.json` matching
`protocol/output-schema.md`. Prose wins where they disagree, and the
disagreement is a review finding.

### Constraints

- No extensions, no modifications, no proposed resolutions. If you find
  yourself building one, put it in Part 6 as an open question.
- No verdict on whether timescape is correct. That is not what this run
  is for and an output containing one is a failed run regardless of which
  way it goes.
- Coverage over depth. A ledger entry with four of ten fields filled and
  six marked unavailable beats a polished entry that quietly omits the
  parameter counts.
- Several seed references are given by author and approximate year on
  purpose. Returning them as complete citations with locators you did not
  retrieve is a run-invalidating failure.
- No preamble, no closing summary. Part 1 starts at the first line.

### Where to look

Primary sources: Wiltshire's own papers for the model and the
Cosmological Equivalence Principle; Buchert for the averaging formalism;
the Green-Wald papers and the Buchert-et-al response for the backreaction
dispute; the published timescape supernova, CMB, and BAO analyses; the
critiques, by name, from the people who made them rather than from
proponents' characterisations of them. Check arXiv listings and citation
graphs for comment papers and replies, which is where the sharpest
objections usually sit and where summaries usually miss them.
