# Run 02 - Adversarial pass over an audit run

Prepend `00-system-adversary.md`. Do not edit this file after a run has
used it; fork it to a new number.

Provide the agent with: the target run's `OUTPUT.md`, `OUTPUT.json`, and
`LEDGER.md` if present; `docs/problem-statement.md`,
`docs/claim-ledger.md`, `docs/rival-approaches.md`,
`protocol/EVIDENCE-RULES.md`, `protocol/output-schema.md`,
`protocol/METHOD.md`, `protocol/EXTENSION-RULES.md`.

Do **not** provide: the target run's `REVIEW.md`, or
`docs/derivation-chain.md` and `docs/objections.md`. The first would
anchor the findings; the other two are the seed the target was supposed to
correct, and an adversary holding them will mistake seed agreement for
verification.

The adversary must run on a fresh context, not a fork of the target run.

---

## Prompt

Attack the audit run named in `META.yaml`. Find where it is wrong and
prove it.

Work the surfaces below in order; they are ordered by how much damage an
error there does. Report `CONFIRMED` before `PLAUSIBLE`, and return zero
findings for a surface when that is what you find.

### S-1. Kind-tag errors

The highest-value surface in this repository. The audit classified each
derivation step as theorem, scheme, postulate, fit, or interpretation, and
the characteristic failure is over-tagging `THEOREM`.

For every step tagged `THEOREM`, establish whether it is one: does it
follow from general relativity with no additional assumption, no choice of
averaging procedure, no choice of foliation, and no physical postulate? A
step that requires a specific scalar averaging scheme is a `SCHEME`. A
step that requires a quasilocal energy interpretation or an equivalence
principle beyond the standard one is a `POSTULATE`. Scalar averaging in
particular carries a foliation choice, so check whether a step was tagged
`THEOREM` while depending on it.

Then the reverse: a step tagged `POSTULATE` or `SCHEME` that is in fact
derivable. Less common, and a real finding, because it understates the
model.

Also check every tag where the target run disagreed with the seed. The
run was invited to correct the seed, which also makes this where it could
have gone wrong with the most confidence.

### S-2. Weight scores that do not hold

Each step carries a 0-3 score for how much of the conclusion collapses if
it fails. Attack these directly:

- Take a step scored 3 and try to construct a version of timescape that
  survives without it. If you can, the score is wrong.
- Take a step scored 0 or 1 and check whether the conclusion actually
  survives its failure.
- Check the independence claim: the audit should have established that the
  backreaction-magnitude question and the clock-effect question are
  independent, so that granting one does not rescue the other. If it
  asserted dependence or independence without argument, that is a finding.

The weight distribution is the main result of Run 01, so an error here
damages more than a citation error does.

### S-3. Ledger entries missing their preconditions

`protocol/EVIDENCE-RULES.md` forbids reporting a comparison statistic
without the dataset, the cuts, the priors, and the free-parameter count on
both sides. Check every ledger entry that reports one.

Then check the counts themselves against the source. A model comparison
computed with different parameter counts on the two models and reported
without that fact is the single most consequential reporting error
available here, because it inverts how much the headline evidence is
worth.

### S-4. The standardisation claim

For each supernova analysis in the ledger, the audit had to establish from
the method section how light-curve standardisation parameters were
treated, and whether a calibration derived under a standard-model
assumption was inherited.

Go to the method sections yourself. This is checkable, it decides how much
the headline evidence is worth, and a run that asserted it without
quoting the method section has asserted the most load-bearing empirical
fact in the audit on no evidence.

If the audit reported this as unestablished, verify that it is genuinely
not establishable from the published text rather than merely not looked
up.

### S-5. Smuggled verdicts

The audit was forbidden from resolving the backreaction-magnitude dispute
or the gauge status of the clock effect. Check for resolution arriving by
means other than a stated verdict:

- Asymmetric space or detail given to the two sides.
- One side's position stated in its own terms and the other's only as
  characterised by opponents.
- Hedged language on one side and plain assertion on the other.
- A `resolved: true` in the JSON without a locator showing the field
  resolved it.
- A conclusion elsewhere in the output that only follows if one side is
  right.

Quote the text and show how the resolution enters. Then check the same in
both directions, per the symmetry rule in the role card.

### S-6. Independence and status inflation

`protocol/EVIDENCE-RULES.md` makes `supported` require confirmation from
outside the proposing group. Check every `supported` status. In a small
field this confirmation is frequently absent, and the failure is to treat
a multi-author paper from one group as independent confirmation.

### S-7. Locators

Fetch every locator. Does it exist, is it the work attributed, does it say
what the run claims at the place cited. One confirmed fabrication caps the
target at 0 on rubric axis 1.

The seed bibliography deliberately contains entries given by author and
approximate year only. Check whether the run returned any of them with a
locator it did not retrieve; the headline Pantheon+ analysis is the one
most likely to have been filled in from memory.

Also check `papers_retrieved` in `META.yaml` against the number of
distinct sources cited as `[SOURCED]`. A large gap is a finding on its
own.

### S-8. The rival comparison

The audit was told to prioritise one comparison: Buchert averaging with
backreaction but without the clock-effect postulate, because it isolates
what the clock effect buys.

Check whether a number was actually established or whether the comparison
was described qualitatively and reported as done. If the audit concluded
no such number exists in the literature, verify that.

### S-9. Smuggled extensions

The audit was forbidden from proposing modifications. Check the open
questions and the scaffold-correction sections for proposals wearing a
question mark. "Could the two-phase split be generalised to a continuum
with the following measure?" is an extension, not a question, and the
gate in `protocol/EXTENSION-RULES.md` applies to it.

### S-10. Prose and JSON divergence

Compare field by field; prose is authoritative. Check that
`objections[].resolved` is `false` throughout unless accompanied by a
`resolution_note` with a locator.

## Deliverables

`OUTPUT.md` containing:

**Part 1 - Findings.** `CONFIRMED` first, ordered by damage, each with the
four required parts from the role card.

**Part 2 - What you verified and found sound.** Named specifically: which
locators you fetched and confirmed, which kind-tags you checked and agree
with, which method sections you read. This part is as important as Part 1,
because it is the only way confidence in this repository's outputs
accumulates. A pass with no Part 2 cannot be built on.

**Part 3 - Your own rubric scores.** 0-3 on each of the five axes in
`protocol/METHOD.md`, one sentence each, formed before reading the
target's self-review.

**Part 4 - Divergence from the self-review.** Read the target's
`REVIEW.md` only now, and report where your scores differ and why.

**Part 5 - The gate decision.** `protocol/EXTENSION-RULES.md` unlocks
extension runs only if the audit scores at least 2 on every rubric axis
and this adversarial pass exists with its findings addressed. State
plainly whether the audit clears the first condition on your scores, and
list the findings that must be addressed before the gate opens. Be
willing to keep the gate shut. An audit that does not clear it is a normal
outcome for a first run and saying so is the correct result.

`OUTPUT.json` with the findings as a list, each carrying the quoted text,
the verdict `CONFIRMED` or `PLAUSIBLE`, the surface id `S-1` to `S-10`,
the damage estimate, your evidence locators, and the corrected statement.
Include your rubric scores and the gate decision as top-level fields.

## Constraints

- Zero findings is a legitimate result. Return it rather than padding.
- No findings that amount to a verdict on timescape, in either direction.
- Do not redo the audit, propose extensions, or fill in work you think
  was missed.
- Do not read the target's `REVIEW.md` before Part 4.
- No preamble. Part 1 starts at the first line.
